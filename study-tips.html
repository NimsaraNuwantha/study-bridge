Study Tips

Small habits make a big difference. Pick one or two tips to try this week.

Time management

Plan your week

  1. List all lectures, deadlines and exams.
  2. Block study sessions in your calendar.
  3. Do the hardest subject when you are most alert.
  4. Review the plan every Sunday.

Stay focused

  • Study in 25-minute sessions with 5-minute breaks.
  • Keep your phone out of reach while studying.
  • Set one clear goal for each session.
  • Get enough sleep, as it helps memory.

Notes and exam preparation

Better notes

  • Write notes in your own words.
  • Use headings, short lists and diagrams.
  • Review notes within 24 hours of the lecture.

Exam preparation

  • Start revising at least two weeks early.
  • Practise with past papers under time limits.
  • Test yourself instead of only re-reading.
  • Study with a friend and explain topics aloud.

Learning during disruptions

Severe weather can cause power cuts and unreliable internet, and universities may move lectures online. A little preparation helps.

Three-stage plan for disrupted learning
StageWhat to do
1. Prepare Download lecture slides, readings and videos while you have power and data. Charge your phone and power bank. Save your lecturers' contact details.
2. During the disruption Study from downloaded or printed material. Check official university notices when you have signal. Message your lecturer if you miss a deadline or class.
3. Recover Watch recorded lectures, catch up on missed work, and ask about extensions or alternative assessments.

Safety first: during storms or floods, follow official instructions. No assignment is worth taking a risk. For weather and disaster updates see the Resources page.

Frequently asked questions

It depends on your course, but many students aim for 2 to 4 focused hours outside lectures. Use the Planner to work out a schedule.

Use any downloaded material first, then contact your lecturer or department once you have a signal. Most universities allow alternative arrangements for genuine disruptions, so ask early.

Start with just five minutes. Breaking a task into small steps makes it easier to begin.

Talk to a friend, a lecturer or your university's student counsellor. You do not have to cope alone.
